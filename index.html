Aviation weather for regional and GFS model sets (v1.2.75)

Aviation weather is now a dedicated field menu containing Visibility, Ceiling height, and Flight category; these fields no longer appear in the Surface weather menu. Flight Category is now selectable for GFSv16 and GFSv17 as well as RRFS/REFS/HRRR, using the same VLIFR/LIFR/IFR/MVFR/VFR palette and METAR colors. It remains excluded from the AI model set. New GFS model plots require the paired converter v1.4.63 to process native VIS and same-valid-time cloud ceiling (AGL) and derive flightcat. GFS categorical difference panels do not subtract category codes.

Aviation analyses: for RRFS/REFS, and for GFS's analysis panel, published URMA fields are preferred, then RTMA, then RAP where available for the exact valid time. In GFS, the GFSv16 F000 model analysis is the final fallback when the observational analysis is unavailable. The analysis panel title identifies the actual selected source. Other GFS analysis plots retain their existing behavior. The converter offers gfs_to_zarr.py --repair-aviation to fill missing aviation fields in previously completed GFS hours without refetching unrelated fields.

Low-track publication and valid-hour status (v1.3.5)

When Data overlays → Low tracks is enabled, an affected MSLP-capable panel without current track information displays a small, nonblocking message. Forecast panels use official gettrk tracks, while F000 analysis panels use a separate experimental F000 diagnostic history. This distinguishes a not-yet-published model/cycle, an unprocessed forecast lead, a downloaded but empty current-time track set, and download errors. The Data overlays menu also summarizes availability across panels. A visible MSLP depression alone does not prove that the independently scheduled NCEP/GFDL gettrk job has produced a track: GFS/AI forecast runs normally wait until the first early checkpoint (default F012), and live plots at F006 can precede those records. A track marker is drawn only when an official fix exists at the exact selected valid hour inside the model's native domain; no cyclone centers are inferred or extrapolated from the map.

Low tracks as a surface Data overlay (v1.3.5)

There is no longer a standalone Low tracks plot. Select MSLP, 2-m Temperature, 2-m Dewpoint, 2-m RH, 10-m Wind or 10-m Gust, then enable Data overlays → Low tracks. Tracks remain available regardless of whether MSLP or the original field is shaded, and do not affect MSLP isobars, H/L/front analysis, source data or difference calculation. The overlay starts off for first-time visitors, remembers explicit preferences, follows the chosen cycle and forecast hour, and appears in image exports when enabled. Forecast-track availability depends on independently published NCEP/GFDL gettrk metadata; F000 analysis histories use separate surface-weather diagnostics. Data overlays distinguishes unavailable from no active tracks. Older links using field=lowtracks open MSLP with tracks enabled.

F000 low-center history (v1.3.5)

Analysis RAP F000 (hourly) and GFSv16 F000 (six-hourly) overlay paths accumulate low-center positions from the selected initialization through the displayed valid time. Independent F000 centers are linked conservatively only across successive available timestamps; missing hours are never bridged. Earlier paths remain visible as history. The source is the experimental surface-weather analysis, not the official GFDL VortexTracker. Missing, incomplete and empty F000 diagnostic series are reported on affected panels and in Data overlays. Publication requires converter v1.4.94.

Difference field interpolation (v1.3.3)

When interpolation is enabled, the GPU now shields signed difference textures from missing-value sentinel contamination. Invalid cells stay fully masked, while display-only texture inputs use neighboring valid values instead of extreme negative missing markers. This addresses spurious thin blue lines near gaps or edges in difference images without changing native data, cursor samples, contour values, exported differences, or your interpolation preference.

Synoptic centers and coastal-front screening (v1.3.3)

The companion converter v1.4.93 now applies stronger, synoptic-scale pressure prominence and multi-direction support before identifying distinct H/L centers, plus coastline-aware frontal screening that rejects shore-parallel land/water thermal gradients without convincing pressure and wind evidence. The browser also spaces H/L labels farther apart when zoomed out, including pressure values. Genuine dynamically supported coastal fronts remain eligible. Automated boundaries are experimental, not official WPC analyses. Published surface-weather GeoJSON regenerates incrementally under the existing independent producer; the official cyclone research archive is unchanged.

Synoptic surface analysis refinements (v1.2.93)

Data overlays lists ordinary weather-layer switches first, followed by a full-width Surface weather analysis section. The companion converter v1.4.91 uses multilevel, contiguous frontal-evidence diagnostics: a candidate must have a coherent thermal gradient and corroborating near-surface pressure-trough, wind shift, or convergence evidence. Broad high-pressure centers are searched at multiple spatial scales. The automated fronts remain experimental guidance, not official WPC boundaries and are not guaranteed to match subjective WPC analyses. Older GeoJSON analyses refresh in the background under the new algorithm; unavailable or empty features remain clearly distinguished in panel status messages.

Surface weather status and defaults (v1.2.92)

The experimental Surface weather analysis overlay now starts OFF, including after upgrading from a version that enabled it automatically. When you turn it on, that choice is remembered for subsequent visits. A panel status message appears above the fullscreen control and distinguishes an unavailable overlay (HTTP 404/410) from access denial (HTTP 401/403), a busy server (HTTP 429), a server error, an invalid GeoJSON response, and a valid analysis with no features. For technical details, hover the panel message or the Data overlays summary to see the error and requested GeoJSON path. The status is omitted from image/video exports.

Surface weather data availability (v1.2.91)

With Surface weather analysis enabled, each eligible model panel reports when its overlay is loading, not yet published for that model/hour, unavailable due to a download failure, or successfully loaded with no detected features. These are different conditions. A published file may contain zero fronts, centers or boundaries, or may contain features hidden by the selected overlay types. The Data overlays menu summarizes the active panels and has a Retry button for missing, failed, or empty frames; retry checks the current published data again. Successful overlays display a feature count in the menu; status messages on the map disappear when features are available. These UI messages do not appear in PNG/GIF/video exports.

Surface weather analysis overlay (v1.2.90)

For MSLP and surface plots that support MSLP, the Data overlays → Surface weather analysis controls display automatically detected high/low centers and pressure values, cold/warm/stationary front candidates, drylines and surface troughs. Symbols follow familiar meteorological map conventions. Only model cycles and valid hours with published GeoJSON are drawn. Frontal positions are objective experimental guidance, not official WPC analyses. Occlusions are drawn only when provided by independently validated features. Exported panel PNGs, multi-panel PNGs and GIF frames include these overlays when active. To populate maps, install the companion converter and enable its surface-weather producer.

Flight Category and enhanced Surface plots (v1.2.71)

Surface weather → Flight Category plots a discrete category derived from the most restrictive of same-hour visibility and ceiling: VLIFR (purple), LIFR (magenta), IFR (red), MVFR (blue), VFR (green). Values at exactly 0.5/1/3/5 statute miles or 200/500/1000/3000 ft AGL follow standard category boundaries; a missing value does not become zero. Available METAR markers use identical category colors and popups show Flight Category with available ceiling/visibility.

For 2-m Temperature, 2-m Dewpoint, 2-m RH, 10-m Wind and 10-m Gust, a shaded-image radio selection switches between the original primary field and MSLP. Independently overlaid MSLP isobars are ON by default on all five fields; toggle them using the MSLP contours checkbox. Individual and multi-panel PNG/GIF exports follow the same selection and contour controls. Terrain is disabled in Globe projection and is switched off when entering Globe; return to 2D to enable it. The original Mixed precipitation colors remain unchanged. Flight Category requires the accompanying converter v1.4.62 to publish the derived model/analysis field.

Visibility, ceiling and RAP/MRMS verification (v1.2.70)

The Surface menu now includes Visibility (mi) and Ceiling height (AGL, ft). On these fields, METAR circles use the same plot color scale; clicking a METAR shows available visibility and ceiling with its time and precipitation type. Recent observations are checked without the previous 30-minute wait and refresh roughly every minute. Analysis precipitation type now uses RAP F000, and Reflectivity with Ptype overlays MRMS reflectivity with same-valid-time RAP F000 categories. Mixed precipitation retains its original yellow/amber presentation. New native fields and observation JSON updates require converter/producer publication—see data requirements. The live-style multi-panel PNG/GIF contours from v1.2.68 are preserved; individual PNGs are unchanged.

AI time-series lines corrected (v1.2.65)

On the AI / GFS / GEFS workspace, point time series now sample F000, F006, F012, and subsequent selectable six-hour leads through F240, connecting adjacent available values and leaving gaps for genuinely unavailable leads. The GFSv16/v17 workspace remains three-hourly, and RRFS/REFS remains hourly. The lines use a visible three-pixel stroke; stored data, field values, and accumulation-bar behavior are unchanged.

AI viewer now has nine panels (v1.2.64)

Existing rows remain AIGFS | AIGEFS Mean | HGEFS Mean and GFSv16 | GEFS Mean | Analysis (GFSv16 F000). The new bottom row is EAGLE Solo (developmental AIGFS) | EAGLE Ensemble (developmental AIGEFS) | HEAGLE (developmental HGEFS). EAGLE Solo has its own independent catalog_eagle_solo.json, published by the paired converter from the supplied NOAA developmental S3 feed. Its catalog may be pending until data are ready. The latter two panels remain labeled placeholders until their source locations are provided; no fields are synthesized. Terrain, tracing, differences and exports retain established behavior on available panels.

Fast GFS/AI wind tracers and 80% terrain default (v1.2.63)

Eligible regular latitude/longitude GFS, AI and analysis wind fields now use a strictly validated direct native U/V lookup, avoiding repeated grid-axis scans for every animated wind tracer. Compatible Lambert grids keep their previously improved path. Density, speed, color, outline, and forecast/terrain display are unchanged; unsupported grids use the original sampler. The browser console command EVUAdvancedMap.getTracerPerformance() now separates fastLatLonPanels from fastLambertPanels. Terrain opacity defaults to 80% when no custom value is saved, and the Terrain right-click Reset action returns it to 80%. User-saved terrain opacity and Image opacity (default 100%) remain independent and unchanged.

Faster RRFS/REFS wind tracers (v1.2.62)

On supported native Lambert U/V grids, wind tracer animation now uses a validated direct-grid lookup instead of scanning thousands of grid-axis coordinates for every particle. The same native wind values, density, speed, colors, thin outline, and forecast/terrain appearance are retained. Mismatched and unsupported grids automatically use the original sampling path. For diagnostics open the browser console and run EVUAdvancedMap.getTracerPerformance() to see fastGridPanels, referenceGridPanels, and averageFrameWorkMs; EVUFastWindSampler.getDiagnostics() reports the last validation. The actual speedup depends on device and visible panels; no change to rendering quality or user settings is intended.

Sounding profile integrity (v1.2.61)

When a full native sounding is unavailable, the existing six-level sparse fallback is labeled as such; it is not the model's actual native vertical resolution. To distinguish truly missing levels from levels intentionally hidden below ground, inspect the T/Td/Wind readout and its new below-ground-removal detail. With the sounding open, EVUSoundingProfiles.getDiagnostics() in the browser console reports the requested model/cycle/hour, native profile store, raw and rendered levels, and missing chunks or an unavailable catalog hour. The profile converter must publish complete native chunks and the profile catalog for every desired model run and forecast hour. See RELEASE_1_2_61.md and the paired Converter v1.4.57 audit instructions. GFS soundings, terrain, wind tracers and export workflows are not changed.

North America-wide DEM coverage (v1.2.60)

Weather-model grids and local DEM tile coverage are separate. Global elevation tiles are installed through z5, but previous z6–9 tiles cover only one CONUS-centered rectangle, so panning over Alaska, the Aleutians, Hawaii, northern Canada, and Greenland may produce terrain 404s. The updated Python installer offers --region-preset north-america, including mainland North America, Greenland, Hawaii (including the Northwestern Hawaiian Islands), Puerto Rico, and the Aleutians on both sides of the date line. A full z0–9 plan contains 58,873 unique tiles (45,413 additional relative to the existing 13,460); make sure sufficient disk space is available. On an Internet-connected installation host, preview with python3 install_terrain_dem.py --dest data/dem --region-preset north-america --max-zoom 9 --global-base-maxzoom 5 --max-tiles 70000 --dry-run. Remove --dry-run and optionally add --workers 8 to install; reuse existing files and publish the new PNGs and manifest (manifest last). The smaller z0–8 option plans 15,762 tiles. The viewer code, weather interpolation, terrain renderer and DEM source warnings are unchanged.

Reset View and regional terrain coverage (v1.2.59)

Reset View now frames native regular-latitude/longitude datasets (GFS, compatible AI and analyses) as well as Lambert datasets without repeating invalid projected-corner warnings. This changes only the camera overview, not model grid coordinates or weather values. If terrain panning yields missing data/dem/6/9/19.png or 6/11/19.png, those tiles are north of 55°N, outside the previous local installation bbox. On the installation host, extend the DEM with python3 install_terrain_dem.py --dest data/dem --bbox -150 15 -65 65 --max-zoom 9 --global-base-maxzoom 5 --workers 8, then publish the added PNGs and updated manifest to the website. The extension reuses existing tiles. This release does not change the separate MapLibre shared-DEM-source warnings.

Smooth terrain shading for continuous Lambert-grid fields (v1.2.55)

With Terrain and Interpolate enabled, continuous Lambert-grid forecast values (including RRFS/REFS and compatible analyses) are now bilinearly interpolated before their values are converted to precipitation/temperature colors. This smooths visible grid-cell boundaries at close zoom and follows the existing interpolation control. The fast, validated native Lambert sampler remains active for matching grids. Precipitation-type categories, other categorical fields, and Interpolate OFF remain native nearest-grid-point displays. The underlying model values, grids, geometry, color scales, wind overlays, difference calculations, and exports are unchanged. Diagnostics: EVUThreeSurface.getStatus() includes lastTextureInterpolationMode (native-bilinear or native-nearest).

RRFS/REFS terrain sampler correction (v1.2.54)

The validated fast Lambert sampler now matches the native autumnplot-gl grid spacing and nearest-grid-point coordinates, including the exact float32 grid cache behavior. The resulting final terrain mesh, color palette and forecast-texture resolution remain unchanged. After rendering, use EVUThreeSurface.getStatus() and EVUFastLambertSampler.getDiagnostics() to verify validated-lambert-row with the autumnplot-grid-extent convention. The 13,460-tile DEM expansion already completed does not need to be rerun for this update.

RRFS/REFS terrain sampling and DEM coverage (v1.2.54)

Terrain retains its full-quality final mesh and weather texture. The accelerated Lambert sampler is validated against each source grid and falls back safely if it cannot match the original field. Optional browser diagnostics: EVUThreeSurface.getStatus() includes lastTextureSamplingMode and lastTextureSamplingReason; EVUFastLambertSampler.getDiagnostics() provides the calibration detail. Resizing may reuse the existing full-quality texture when its geographic coverage still matches. For persistent z6 terrain tile 404s outside the installed region, broaden the local DEM installation as documented in the complete User Guide and release notes; the viewer ZIP does not contain elevation tiles.

Current model sets and upper-air options (v1.2.60)

Full-quality terrain optimization (v1.2.54): Validated native Lambert row sampling avoids costly per-pixel grid projections without changing texture resolution, relief, or color palettes; unsupported grids keep the reference sampler. For measured browser timing run EVUThreeSurface.getStatus() to inspect lastTextureSamplingMode, lastTextureSampleMs, and lastTextureColorMs. Local DEM 404s outside the original regional tile coverage require installing additional elevation PNGs with the updated install_terrain_dem.py script, not just replacing viewer code.

Terrain rendering (v1.2.51): The low-quality temporary terrain preview and extra DEM-readiness wait introduced in recent releases have been removed. The established direct high-detail Three.js terrain renderer is restored, with the original mesh and forecast-color sampling settings; DEM tiles may still refine the terrain as they arrive. This change restores predictable image quality rather than promising a new speed improvement. The expanded-panel geographic footprint, wind tracers, video/PNG/GIF exports, and other controls remain unchanged. To diagnose a slow high-detail draw, use EVUThreeSurface.getStatus() in the browser console and compare its mesh, texture, projection and draw timings.

AI / GFS / GEFS offers 250/300-hPa Height/Wind, 500-hPa Height/Vorticity, and 700/850/925-hPa Height/Temperature/Moisture and 1000-hPa Height/Temperature, subject to published source fields. AI 2-m dewpoint and specific humidity are intentionally omitted. Use the shaded image radio choices within Data overlays to switch Wind/Height (250/300), Vorticity/Height (500), Temperature/Height/Dewpoint/Specific humidity (700/850/925), or Temperature/Height (1000). At 850 hPa, Wind speed can be shaded and contoured independently of Temperature, Height, Dewpoint, Specific humidity, and Wind barbs. The MSLP plot also offers MSLP or 850-hPa temperature as a shaded image and difference scalar. This also selects the GFS or on-demand difference scalar; dewpoint and specific-humidity contours remain independent.

Wind tracers use a complete native U/V pair at the same forecast hour and initialization, including a compatible companion data store if available. Wind tracers retain their speed-based palette with a thin translucent contrasting outer keyline. Precipitation verification is shared across all model sets: CCPA first, Stage IV backup, MRMS second backup, requiring a complete unmixed accumulation. Inventory refresh runs approximately every three minutes while the tab is active.

Runtime libraries and licensing (MapLibre GL JS, autumnplot.gl and Three.js) are documented in the complete PDF guide and third-party acknowledgments. Required terrain, map and observation data-source attribution remains visible when applicable.

Viewer version 1.1.84

Missing-data display: All scalar plots now leave missing cells transparent so the map background or terrain remains visible. GFS 10:1 snowfall also leaves zero and trace amounts unshaded; valid zeros in other fields remain data. This applies to standard map images and exports.

The OMD EVU Model Viewer displays weather model forecasts and available verifying analyses side by side. The panels share a geographic view, making it easier to compare different models and forecast hours. The default layout includes RRFS, HRRR, REFS members, REFS Mean, and an Analysis panel.

Getting started

  1. Choose a Model set, Field, and model Cycle (initialization time, in UTC).
  2. Choose an F-hour to display the forecast. For example, F024 is 24 hours after initialization. Each panel displays its valid time.
  3. Zoom or pan any map to focus on an area. The other panels follow the same view.
  4. Use Previous, Next, Play/Pause, and Speed to move through the forecast. A panel indicates when the requested data are unavailable.

The Field menu groups related weather variables, including radar, precipitation type, surface weather, snowfall, upper air, severe-weather parameters, and GFS marine weather.

Expand a panel: The expand button now preserves the original small panel's geographic area as closely as the larger window's aspect ratio allows. The center, bearing and pitch remain unchanged. If the enlarged window is proportionally wider or taller, the extra screen space may display modest additional geography along that dimension. Zooming or panning while expanded is retained when you return to the comparison grid. This applies to every model set, analysis panel and 2D/Globe view.

Model sets: RRFS/REFS contains nine panels and supports hourly forecast lead navigation and an optional floating two-panel difference. GFSv16/GFSv17 contains six panels (two forecasts, matching GFSv16 F000 analysis, and three signed differences), with three-hourly forecast lead navigation. GFS does not offer 1-hour precipitation or snowfall accumulations. F000 verifying analysis exists only at matching synoptic valid times. The viewer uses published native-grid difference stores when available and otherwise computes compatible differences on demand from the underlying GFS source grids or aligned verification fields, preserving the existing availability rules. Controls for maps, shading, overlays, point inspection, and exporting follow the same interface, subject to each field’s actual availability.

Map controls and shortcuts

3D perspective: Ctrl + drag (or right-drag) tilts and rotates synchronized 2D maps. Terrain starts OFF and uses a locally hosted 3-D DEM when installed. The former bundled 2-D raster fallback has been removed. Run install_terrain_dem.py to fetch the same-origin elevation tiles if not yet installed. Turning Terrain on leaves Image opacity unchanged (100% by default). Surface and near-surface weather fields retain their forecast palette with neutral relief shadows at full opacity; upper-air fields at full opacity obscure terrain. You can adjust Image opacity at any time without losing the setting. Right-click Terrain for independent terrain shading, vertical exaggeration and sunlight (0× flat, 1× natural scale, up to 20×). State/Country and independently enabled County outlines remain drawn above the relief. Wind tracers (off by default) draw speed-directed atmospheric streamlines for eligible fields, including non-swell GFS wave plots with 10 m winds. Right-click the wind-tracer label (which names its active level) for light/dark-specific color, thickness and density. For dense RRFS/REFS comparisons the viewer reuses projected tracer endpoints, restores full selected density when possible, and temporarily reduces particle work if the device is overloaded; barbs and tracers pause during an active camera gesture and restart when the view settles. The 3-D terrain compositor reuses unchanged full-resolution weather textures after DEM/lighting updates, with no change to sampled forecast values or field palettes. The interim terrain preview now chooses its sample density from the source grid spacing and the current map scale rather than a fixed low-resolution preview across all datasets. Fine native grids receive more detail within an interactive workload limit; the final terrain quality remains unchanged. Use Shortcuts for the keyboard, mouse, hover, touch and PDF reference.

Right-click: time series and sounding

On a desktop, right-click a location on a model or analysis map; on a touch device, press and hold that location for about 0.6 seconds without moving your finger. Then select one of these tools. Both open a floating chart window that you can move or resize. Use the download icon in the chart header to save a PNG. Open soundings and point time series change their chart colors immediately when the viewer switches between light and dark modes; saved PNGs reflect the active theme without reloading the chart data.

Time series plots the current field at the clicked location across the full forecast cycle. The AI / GFS / GEFS model set now samples at its selectable six-hour cadence through F240; GFSv16/v17 uses three-hourly samples through F240, and RRFS/REFS keeps its hourly time series. Consecutive available values are connected by clear lines, while genuinely missing nominal forecast leads remain gaps. It stays tied to the chosen model, field, location, and cycle, even if you subsequently change what is displayed in the viewer. Instantaneous quantities and run-to-date totals are shown as lines; time-period precipitation and snowfall accumulations (such as 3-, 6-, and 24-hour totals in GFS, with 1-hour totals also available in RRFS/REFS) are shown as bars. Rolling accumulation intervals can overlap, so adjacent bars should not be added together. Unavailable hours are not zero. The Y-axis uses rounded, whole-number ticks for most fields, retaining decimals for precipitation and other quantities where sub-unit differences matter. The data-availability note is separated from the forecast lead-time axis label, and the vertical/horizontal grid lines are visible in either display theme. It may take a moment to load the full series; progress is shown in the chart window. For Reflectivity with Ptype, the series plots reflectivity (dBZ), not the precipitation-type category.

Sounding displays a Skew-T / Log-P sounding at the clicked location, with red temperature, green dewpoint, correctly oriented meteorological wind barbs, a roomier wind hodograph, and a model diagnostics panel that includes both 0–1 km and 0–6 km bulk shear (when published). When available, it uses the newer pressure-level profiles collected by the converter, extending up to 100 hPa. The diagnostics box samples model-grid CAPE, helicity, and 0–1 km / 0–6 km bulk shear values when published. These values are sampled independently from the published model grids, not calculated from the profile winds. Basic surface and level-summary information is also displayed. The Skew-T uses a practical −50°C to +50°C near-surface temperature axis and a header spaced above the plot. Hodograph wind-speed ring labels are kept clear of the cardinal directions. The dry-adiabat and mixing-ratio guides remain visible in both light and dark mode, while the valid date/time and latitude/longitude stay anchored within the chart during refreshes. The sounding updates automatically when the forecast hour or cycle changes, including during playback. Its chosen location and model remain the same.

An approximate most-unstable (MU) parcel trace appears as a dashed gold line when the native profile has enough temperature and dewpoint levels. It selects the highest equivalent potential temperature in the lowest 300 hPa of usable moisture data, lifting dry adiabatically to the LCL and approximately moist adiabatically above. Its starting pressure and LCL are noted below the diagnostics, and it is omitted for sparse or incomplete moisture profiles. It is a qualitative approximation: no parcel-derived CAPE/CIN or authoritative convective diagnostics are implied.

The sounding window reports how many temperature, dewpoint, and wind levels are available. REFS Mean uses its own operational ensemble-mean profile, including time-lagged members, but has relatively few available pressure levels. REFS member profiles may also have gaps. RAP analysis soundings are available at their corresponding hourly valid times when published.

Sounding limitations: Full profiles may not yet be available for older cases or every forecast hour. In that situation the viewer uses the earlier, limited-level sounding when possible, and labels it as a sparse fallback. Profile data are sampled from a coarser geographic grid than the maps, so they may not represent local terrain or sharp weather boundaries. Some diagnostics may be unavailable, and any shown values are only as good as the published fields for that source and hour. With a compatible converter, native surface pressure allows below-ground isobaric levels to be excluded, and native 2-m temperature and dewpoint anchor the profile at the ground. Older profiles without surface pressure are clearly identified as not terrain-screened; missing levels are not invented. These soundings are best used for visual case-study exploration, not operational parcel diagnostics or safety-critical decisions. Desktop right-click and mobile touch-and-hold use the same data sources and open the same charts. On small mobile screens, the chart occupies most of the available screen width; the header has Close and Save controls.

Fields, precipitation type, and snowfall

Updraft helicity analysis comparison: For the 1-hour model UH maximum, the Analysis panel displays the radar-derived MRMS mid-level (3–6 km AGL) 60-minute rotation track, ending at the selected valid time. For accumulated model UH, the Analysis panel displays the gridpoint maximum of successive 60-minute MRMS tracks from the beginning of the forecast through the selected valid time. The accumulated MRMS analysis is unavailable unless every required hourly track has been published. Model UH (m² s⁻²) and radar rotation track (×10⁻³ s⁻¹) are different physical quantities; the viewer provides two independently labeled colorbars, including on all-panel PNG/GIF exports. This is a qualitative comparison of rotating-storm signatures, not observed UH.

Precipitation analysis selection: Every model set uses shared verification data in the priority CCPA → Stage IV → MRMS, with availability and latency handled automatically. A complete native accumulation is preferred when appropriate; otherwise a total is assembled from complete, non-overlapping intervals from one source, quality pass, and grid. Never mix providers or fill gaps with zero. MRMS remains the source for radar reflectivity and precipitation rate; source labels identify the actual verification shown.

Precipitation-type labels are model-specific: HRRR is Derived precipitation type, RRFS and REFS members are Dominant precipitation type, and REFS Mean is Ensemble dominant precipitation type. These exact terms appear in panel titles and per-panel PNG/GIF labels. The comparison-wide legend uses the neutral description Precipitation type (model-specific) because all models share one categorical color scale.

REFS Mean PMM products: composite reflectivity is displayed as PMM composite reflectivity. Accumulated precipitation is labeled PMM accumulated precipitation when the published field comes from the PMM precipitation source. The viewer does not infer PMM from a generic ensemble-mean field; older data stores may need the converter's targeted PMM precipitation repair before they contain the correct source data.

Reflectivity with Ptype appears below Precipitation type in the Radar menu. It colors model reflectivity by forecast precipitation type: green progressing into yellow/orange for stronger rain echoes, blue for snow, pink for freezing rain, purple for sleet, and amber for mixed precipitation. Both reflectivity and precipitation type must be available for the chosen hour.

Precipitation rate is displayed in inches per hour (in/hr), including map values, the colorbar, point time series, and PNG/GIF exports. Stored values are unchanged.

Optional grid interpolation: use the Interpolate checkbox beside Image opacity to apply GPU bilinear filtering to continuous scalar-field shading. It is on by default for eligible GFS atmospheric fields, but off by default for GFS Marine weather and RRFS/REFS. Marine, GFS atmospheric, and RRFS/REFS interpolation choices are saved independently in the current browser. This blends the four surrounding grid values for display only; it does not change stored Zarr values, point sampling, time series, sounding data, contours, or calculations. Categorical precipitation type and the hybrid Reflectivity with Ptype field retain nearest-neighbor rendering so category boundaries and codes are not blended. PNG/GIF exports use the same setting as the live panels.

Snowfall and precipitation fields include hourly, multi-hour, and accumulated options where available. Some totals need a complete sequence of earlier data, so a missing input can make a later accumulation unavailable. Missing data are not treated as zero.

GFS 10:1 snowfall method: Select WEASD Change (default, same positive SWE-gain calculation for v16 and v17) or Snow Precipitation (native v17 TSNOWP only). Panel titles abbreviate the default as SWE increase. The v17−v16 WEASD difference uses each model’s separate positive gain; the Snow Precipitation option does not offer an incompatible v16 difference. For GFS snowfall, the Analysis panel uses NOHRSC observations at valid time. The two forecast-minus-NOHRSC difference panels remain unavailable pending spatial regridding.

For NOHRSC snowfall in the Analysis panel, 6-hour analyses are generally valid at 00/06/12/18 UTC and 24-hour analyses at 00/12 UTC. A 48-hour total uses two available 24-hour periods; the accumulated total adds consecutive 6-hour periods beginning after model initialization. There are no native 1-hour or 3-hour NOHRSC plots. Small, isolated snowfall artifacts are filtered from the map shading, without changing reported values.

GFS marine weather: The Marine weather menu in GFS comparison mode contains six wave-height and wave-period scalar products from GFSv16 and GFSv17. Non-swell wave plots default to 10 m Wind barbs ON (real GFS 10-m U/V) and independent Wave direction propagation arrows OFF. Swell-only plots have no 10-m wind barbs and their Swell direction arrows are initially OFF. Native wave-direction records describe propagation and are not meteorological wind barbs. Right-click anywhere in the direction toggle label (Wave direction, Wind-wave direction, or Swell direction) to customize direction color, thickness, and density separately from meteorological wind barbs; settings also apply to exported overlays. The v16 wave source is public AWS GRIB2 and the v17 wave source is local GRIB2. Wave source coverage is not identical to the atmospheric domain, so map regions outside wave coverage remain transparent/missing. Matching scalar wave products can be compared in the GFS difference panels. Circular wave directions themselves are not subtracted, and maximum individual wave height is not included because availability for both versions has not been established.

Adjust the numerical colorbar

You can change the displayed range on most numerical colorbars without changing the underlying values.

  1. Click the numerical colorbar to open the color-scale editor.
  2. Move the Minimum and Maximum sliders, or type precise values into their adjacent entry boxes.
  3. Select Apply to update the shading, Cancel (or Escape) to leave it unchanged, or Reset default to restore the original range. After applying a custom range, use the circular-arrow reset icon beside the colorbar title to restore defaults without opening the editor.

The full color palette stretches across the selected interval. For fields with a transparent near-zero interval, that single interval remains centered on physical zero as the minimum and maximum change; it does not drift with the original palette or create a second blank band. The new scale applies to panels showing that field and is included in exports. The numerical min/max override remains while stepping through forecast hours but resets when the page is reloaded. Right-click any numerical colorbar (or click the shared colorbar) to upload a custom .pal table. Imported palettes are saved per field in browser storage, included in normal panel PNG/GIF exports, and restored after refresh; use Restore built-in colors to remove them. .pal values must use the displayed field units. CAPE colorbars use familiar round labels (such as 500, 1000, and 1500 J/kg). Categorical precipitation-type legends cannot be adjusted this way.

Overlays and transparency

The Shear & helicity field group displays 0–1/0–6 km bulk shear and 0–1/0–3 km storm-relative helicity (SRH). Default color scales are 0–60 kt for 0–1 km and 0–90 kt for 0–6 km; use the numerical colorbar to change them. On bulk shear, the Shear barbs checkbox displays the upper-minus-lower wind vector difference as conventional wind barbs, with 5/10/50-knot markings. Their shafts point opposite the downshear direction, following wind-barb orientation conventions; the barbs describe shear, not wind at a single level. On SRH, the Storm motion barbs checkbox controls native Bunkers right-moving storm-motion barbs, also in knots. All overlays follow synchronized panels, pan/zoom operations, and panel PNG exports, and the severe-weather checkbox state is remembered independently of other wind overlays.

The overlays require native U/V components in the published Zarr catalog for that model and hour. If a source omits them, the corresponding overlay is absent rather than simulated. The RAP analysis requires the latest converter and missing-field recovery for older analysis hours to publish native 0–1 km and 0–6 km shear components. Native RAP 0–1 km components are available in Converter v1.4.1 when provided in the source GRIB inventory. A missing barb overlay does not imply missing scalar shading.

Depending on the field, you can enable height or pressure contours, wind barbs, and contours of the shaded field. Numerical CAPE, shear, storm-relative helicity, updraft helicity, also support shaded-field contours, with conventional rounded value intervals that become finer at close zoom. Categorical precipitation-type fields do not support these numerical contours. For 500-hPa absolute-vorticity shading, RRFS and individual REFS members use a modest display-only smooth, while HRRR and REFS Mean use an even lighter smooth to make plots easier to compare. The stored fields and point-sampled values are unchanged. Small, field-specific values near zero are transparent where a clear background is meteorologically useful: below 10 J/kg for CAPE, below 2 kt for wind speed/components and bulk shear, within ±10 m² s⁻² for SRH, within ±5 m² s⁻² for updraft helicity, within ±5 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ for 500-hPa absolute vorticity, and below 1% for snow cover/frozen-precipitation percentage. The exact neutral interval is tied to physical zero and remains a single interval when the numerical colorbar range changes. Relative humidity remains shaded down to 0% because very dry air is a meaningful meteorological signal. Difference maps also become transparent close to zero. This lets the underlying map background show through in dark and light modes, without changing the meteorological data. Temperature and MSLP shading remain continuous because zero is not a no-weather threshold for those fields. Live height and MSLP contours use slightly thicker, clean single strokes without a contrasting halo; the existing export contour appearance is unchanged. Dark-mode 500-hPa vorticity maps use brighter aqua geographic outlines by default to improve contrast with their vorticity colors, while custom outline styles remain in control. The available overlay checkboxes share compact rows when space permits and wrap when necessary. Report counts appear below the switches. The available options appear with the field controls. Use Image opacity to fade the weather shading without fading geographic outlines, contours, wind barbs, or observation markers. Applicable overlays are also included when exporting plots.

Weather observations and storm reports

On all Radar & precip. type fields, enable METAR to display colored circles or mPING to display triangles. Their colors indicate reported rain, snow, freezing rain, sleet, or mixed precipitation. Reports are usually selected from within about 30 minutes of the forecast valid time.

On supported composite-reflectivity, shear/helicity, or severe-weather maps, enable SPC reports to display preliminary tornado (red), wind (blue), and hail (green) circles. The selected reports are shared by all active panels, including RRFS, HRRR, REFS members, REFS Mean, and Analysis, for the common forecast valid time. Select a report to see any available details. Reports are hidden by default.

On all Radar & precip. type, Shear & helicity, and Instability & severe storms fields, enable GLM lightning to display satellite-observed lightning flash centroids from the preceding 60 minutes, ending at the forecast valid hour. The age colors indicate 0–15 min (pink), 15–30 min (orange), 30–45 min (cyan), and 45–60 min (violet) before that valid hour. The selected overlay remains enabled across fields and page reloads. The flash locations are satellite-detected optical lightning, not individual cloud-to-ground strikes; age is measured relative to the model valid hour, not the current wall-clock time. Choose a lightning-bolt marker to see its timestamp and originating satellite. Published GLM observation hours must be generated by the companion collector; if data are missing, the control reports GLM data unavailable, without implying there was no lightning. Converter 1.4.13 applies the configured retention period to unprotected local and published hours while preserving valid hours covered by protected historical cases. Age colors apply to live maps and panel PNG/GIF exports.

Limitations: Reports may be delayed, incomplete, revised, or absent. Future forecast hours cannot yet have verifying observations. A blank area does not mean no weather occurred. Some accumulated severe-weather fields display reports over a longer period than the most recent hour.

Compare two panels

In RRFS/REFS mode, select the comparison checkboxes beside two compatible panel titles to open a Difference map. In GFS mode, the three difference panels are already included. When a published native-grid difference store is unavailable, the viewer calculates the compatible comparison on demand from the underlying GFSv16/v17 native fields. All difference panels show shaded signed differences and geography without meteorological contours, wind barbs, height isolines, or temperature overlays, irrespective of the normal panel toggles. For a floating RRFS/REFS comparison, the result is first selected panel minus second selected panel. On opening, that floating difference window adjusts its map area to match the selected panel's aspect ratio and geographic footprint, so both horizontal and vertical extents agree as closely as the screen permits. The floating window can be resized freely afterward. Counties are controlled by the independent Counties checkbox at every zoom level, including the floating difference window. Difference PNG exports preserve the checkbox state at capture time. Standard unrotated 2D difference-map PNGs also use a faster projection path without changing image resolution, field sampling, color scales or geographic accuracy; Globe and rotated views retain exact per-pixel projection. Saved difference maps use the same plot-area geometry as individual panel PNGs at the selected output size, reducing differences in geographic crop. Fit and Fill still determine whether edges are shown or cropped, and independently resized or zoomed maps retain their chosen view. Difference-map colorbars use whole-number labels for most fields; fractional labels are retained for small precipitation and other quantities requiring sub-unit precision. The same ticks appear in exported difference PNGs. The difference uses fixed field-specific near-zero transparent ranges and consistent signed colorbar limits through the forecast period. The difference follows changes to the forecast hour and synchronized panel view; you can zoom the floating map independently for close inspection. Use the window's download icon to save a PNG. Incompatible or missing fields cannot be compared.

Save PNGs and animated GIFs

Use a panel's download icon to export one plot, or Export all panels for a comparison image or animation. All exported PNGs and GIFs receive a unique UTC timestamp and short suffix in their filenames, so repeated downloads keep distinct names instead of browser-added (1) and (2) suffixes. Choose the image proportions (16:9 or 4:3) and whether to Fit the entire map area or Fill the frame (which can crop edges). Titles, times, legends, and applicable overlays are included. Exported METAR, mPING, and SPC Local Storm Report markers are slightly enlarged for presentation readability; on-screen marker sizes do not change. Geographic outlines in PNG and GIF exports retain their separate two-tone outline treatment: a contrasting casing with a themed inner stroke. Live maps use one clean stroke without the export halo. This keeps coastlines and political boundaries visible over both dark and bright weather shading in either viewer theme while preserving a cleaner appearance than the older export-specific treatment. This applies to individual, all-panel, and difference-map exports. County visibility is controlled by its checkbox (not zoom) in live maps and PNG/GIF captures; exports honor that checkbox.

Panel video recording: Expand a model panel using its lower-right expand button, then select the panel’s upper-right save button and choose Record expanded panel (video). The browser asks permission to share a tab; choose this tab, not a window or the entire screen. A three-second countdown begins after panel-only capture is established. The floating Stop & save control ends recording and downloads the video in a browser-supported format. The video records the expanded panel, including zooming/panning, live terrain, tracers, overlays, and colorbar. Their effects on the map, including terrain and wind tracers, are captured. Data availability and troubleshooting

  • A panel says missing: The selected model, variable, hour, or verifying analysis may not be available. Try another hour or source. Some accumulated totals require every contributing period.
  • An analysis is missing: Its valid time may not be supported, the analysis may not be published yet, or an earlier required period may be missing. RAP is an hourly model analysis and is only shown for supported fields at valid hours that have been collected and published. The site administrator can check a specific case with the RAP diagnostic page.
  • Changes do not appear after deployment: Refresh the page to load the latest viewer.
  • A plot or map fails to load: The viewer includes a Diagnostics page for technical troubleshooting; contact the site maintainer if the issue persists.

The viewer is intended for forecast evaluation and comparison, not for issuing official warnings. All displayed times are UTC.

Sounding surface wind vectors

Native soundings use 2-m temperature and dewpoint and 10-m U/V wind at the model's native surface-pressure anchor, in addition to the available native isobaric levels. The levels readout counts a surface wind level only when both 10-m vector components are present. This is a display anchor at surface pressure, not a claim that 10-m wind was measured at 2-m height. Older profile stores need the converter's surface-only upgrade before the new level is available. Below-ground pressure levels remain excluded.

Sounding-chart footnotes explain that the MU parcel uses an approximate pseudo-adiabatic path and does not compute CAPE/CIN; the terrain-screening note reports whether native surface pressure was available. These footnotes appear on separate fitted lines beneath the right-hand diagnostics card. The Skew-T axis tilt has also been moderated slightly.
