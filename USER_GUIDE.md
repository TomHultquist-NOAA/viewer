## Getting started

1. Choose a **Model set**, **Field**, and model **Cycle** (initialization time, in UTC).
2. Choose an **F-hour** to display the forecast. For example, F024 is 24 hours after initialization. Each panel displays its valid time.
3. Zoom or pan any map to focus on an area. The other panels follow the same view. In 2D, zooming out is not limited by the model-grid extent; the map may display unshaded areas outside the data.
4. Use **Previous**, **Next**, **Play/Pause**, and **Speed** to move through the forecast. A panel indicates when the requested data are unavailable.

The Field menu groups related weather variables, including radar, precipitation type, surface weather, snowfall, upper air, severe-weather parameters, and GFS marine weather.

**Derived snowfall and method radio controls:** The Winter Weather menu provides **Snowfall (10:1 SLR)** and **Snowfall (snow depth change)** for 3-, 6-, 24-, and 48-hour periods and cycle-to-date accumulations. When **Snowfall (10:1 SLR)** is selected in either model set, two compact radio controls appear: **WEASD Change** and **Snow Precipitation**. **WEASD Change is the default for both GFSv16 and GFSv17.** It takes positive net snowpack water-equivalent (`weasd`) gain over the selected period, multiplies by 10, and converts liquid mm to snowfall inches. The abbreviated panel suffix is **SWE increase**. This is a *10:1 estimate from net SWE change*, not a direct measurement of new snowfall precipitation; melting, compaction, sublimation, and existing snowpack may affect it. Both versions use the same algorithm. The v17−v16 comparison is calculated by differencing the two separately derived positive SWE increases on compatible grids, not by clipping a precomputed WEASD difference. Both GFS models must provide SWE at the period endpoints, including F000 for cycle-to-date.

**Snow Precipitation** uses cumulative `tsnowp` (liquid equivalent of precipitation falling as snow) and a fixed 10:1 SLR, but is available **only for GFSv17**. GFSv16 explicitly reports that TSNOWP is unavailable; the v17−v16 snowfall difference is unavailable in that mode rather than mixing incomparable variables. The radio choice is stored locally across refreshes when permitted and included in shared viewer links.

**GFS snowfall analysis:** When a derived GFS snowfall product is selected, the GFS analysis panel uses the existing NOHRSC observations at the matching **valid UTC time**, not GFSv16 F000. Native 6-hour and 24-hour NOHRSC products are displayed where available. A 48-hour analysis uses two adjacent 24-hour products, and cycle-to-date snowfall sums complete 6-hour observations since the selected model initialization. There is **no independent 3-hour NOHRSC snowfall analysis**. Regions outside NOHRSC coverage remain blank/transparent. NOHRSC's observed snowfall depth is displayed in inches and is not converted by the forecast 10:1 SLR. The two GFS forecast-minus-NOHRSC difference panels remain unavailable for snowfall until geographic resampling and quantity compatibility are validated; the GFSv17−v16 WEASD difference remains available. This is an intentional limitation, not a false zero.

**Snowfall (snow depth change)** subtracts the earlier instantaneous `snod` from the later value, converts to inches, and clips negative changes to zero for snowfall display. Missing and effectively zero net changes are *transparent* on the map, showing the selected map background rather than artificial neutral shading. Snowfall map, sampling, and time-series values are nonnegative; separately calculated forecast differences can remain signed. The net-depth-change calculation is not direct measured snowfall. No hourly periods are interpolated, and unsupported one-hour GFS accumulations are not offered. GFS snowfall accumulation analyses use NOHRSC for supported 6-/24-/48-hour and cycle-to-date periods. GFS menus omit unsupported native `ASNOW`, Snow cover, and updraft helicity fields; their regional-model counterparts remain available.

**Model sets:** RRFS/REFS contains nine panels and supports hourly forecast lead navigation and an optional floating two-panel difference. GFSv16/GFSv17 contains six panels (two forecasts, matching GFSv16 F000 analysis, and three precomputed differences), with three-hourly forecast lead navigation. GFS does not offer 1-hour precipitation or snowfall accumulations. For non-snow fields, F000 verifying analysis exists only at matching synoptic valid times. Snowfall verification uses NOHRSC; those observed products do not cover the complete model domain. All six panels depend on published catalogs and Zarr stores. Controls for maps, shading, overlays, point inspection, and exporting follow the same interface, subject to each field’s actual availability.

## 3D perspective, elevation relief, and wind tracers

**Ctrl + mouse drag** tilts and rotates a 2D map into an oblique 3D perspective, even when Terrain is off. The map camera remains synchronized across active panels. Enabling Terrain **does not automatically change camera pitch or bearing**; the user can tilt and rotate the map independently. The **Reset icon beside Map overlays** returns to the default overview with zero bearing and pitch. Right-click **Terrain** for two additional FIAB-inspired controls: **Sunlight direction** (0–360° clockwise from north; default 315°, northwestern light) and **Hillshade strength** (0–100%; default 75%). The existing Globe projection is separate; Terrain is currently available in the 2D projection only. Toggling Terrain preserves camera orientation and Image opacity.

**Terrain** is OFF by default and now requires the real self-hosted Terrarium DEM: the old bundled 2-D relief has been removed. Install the DEM once with `python3 install_terrain_dem.py --max-zoom 9 --workers 8` on an Internet-connected machine or server. If installing elsewhere, copy `data/dem/` to the website; the browser then needs no access to external DEM sites. Test outlines & terrain** in `diagnostics.html` to validate the manifest and a representative tile.

**Reset View and northward DEM coverage (v1.2.59):** Reset View fits the actual forecast-domain bounds whether the selected dataset is native Lambert (RRFS/REFS/HRRR) or regular latitude/longitude (GFS, eligible AI and analysis products). Global regular grids use a single-world map extent; a malformed catalog uses a safe fallback camera extent rather than repeating a console warning for every panel. This affects the initial map camera only; grid coordinates and weather values do not change. If Chrome reports `data/dem/6/9/19.png` or `data/dem/6/11/19.png` as 404 while panning, those tiles lie around **55.78–58.81°N**, beyond the previously installed DEM regional maximum latitude of **55°N**. On the internet-connected installation host, rerun:

```bash
python3 install_terrain_dem.py --dest data/dem \
  --bbox -150 15 -65 65 --max-zoom 9 \

```

The operation is incremental: of 18,011 planned tiles, the earlier 13,460 are reused if present and intact; roughly 4,551 additional high-zoom tiles are required. Then copy the updated `data/dem/` tree to the viewer website, publishing `manifest.json` last. The viewer ZIP does not contain these terrain PNGs. Coverage is still regional above zoom 5; panning outside the selected bbox may request other absent tiles, so choose a wider bbox if those areas matter. MapLibre warnings about using one DEM source for color relief, hillshade and 3-D terrain are unrelated and are not changed in this release.

**Right-click Terrain** for independently saved light/dark **terrain opacity** (0–100%, default 80%) and **vertical exaggeration** (0–15×, default 8×). **0×** gives a flat mesh, **1×** depicts native elevation scale, and **10×** strongly exaggerates relief. Changes propagate to all live maps without redownloading tiles. Opacity controls elevation coloring and hillshading, not model-shading opacity. The **Image opacity** slider controls forecast images; switching Terrain on or off never changes it. The default terrain opacity is **80%**, and an explicitly saved custom value remains in place until the user changes it. At 100% Image opacity, opaque field pixels conceal terrain below; transparent and missing-value pixels can still expose the underlying DEM. Weather layers remain ordered above relief layers; geographic lines remain above forecast shading. The native GPU weather renderer handles terrain draping for supported grids; other plot types and exports may still use the shared legacy plotting code. Inspect live browser output when deploying a new model grid or adjusting palettes.

**RRFS/REFS full-quality terrain acceleration and local DEM tiles (v1.2.52):** A validated Lambert native-grid row sampler can now speed up the detailed terrain weather texture without lowering its sampling resolution or changing its palette. It is selected from grid geometry and validation, not the model name, and falls back to the established sampler when those checks do not pass. The browser console command `EVUThreeSurface.getStatus()` now shows `lastTextureSamplingMode` (`validated-lambert-row` or `reference`), `lastTextureSampleMs`, and `lastTextureColorMs` in addition to mesh and GPU timings. The revised `install_terrain_dem.py` defaults to worldwide low-zoom coverage through z5; to fill missing PNGs you must rerun it with your existing regional extent and maximum zoom, or install on another machine and copy the resulting `data/dem/` directory to the server. Updating the viewer ZIP alone will not provide missing tiles. See `RELEASE_1_2_52.md`.

The viewer returns directly to the established v1.2.46 Three.js high-detail terrain compositor, preserving its original geographic padding, full-resolution forecast-color texture, mesh density, and terrain lighting behavior. MapLibre DEM tiles may still load progressively and cause a later detail refresh. This version restores the prior visual baseline rather than promising faster high-resolution terrain. **RRFS/REFS terrain and wind-overlay responsiveness (v1.2.46):** The terrain compositor now retains an unchanged, native-resolution weather color texture when refreshing the DEM mesh or lighting at the same geographic coverage. It also avoids redundant latitude/longitude coordinate calculations, without reducing model field resolution or changing terrain colors. Animated wind tracers reuse the projected endpoint from the previous segment and stagger new particles after a map change. In a heavy multi-panel view, an adaptive CPU budget may temporarily draw fewer particles to keep the interaction responsive; the selected density preference is not modified and the target count returns when resources allow. Optional performance counters are available in the browser console via `EVUThreeSurface.getStatus()` and `EVUAdvancedMap.getTracerPerformance()`; they do not require any user configuration.

**Wind tracers** are also **off by default**. Where actual model U/V components are available at the selected forecast hour, the viewer draws animated wind speed-colored, time-faded streaks advected with the wind. The trail lengths are relative to the displayed wind speeds and remain stable over time instead of growing indefinitely. Animation is directionally advected by the native U/V field (m/s); on-screen particle displacement is proportional to wind speed at the same map scale. A consistent 0–90+ kt color ramp distinguishes slower cyan/green winds, stronger yellow/orange winds and very strong red/pink/magenta winds. A narrow opposing edge keeps tracers visible over variable image palettes. Upper-air fields use their corresponding pressure-level winds; suitable near-surface fields use 10-m U/V. Wind tracers are also available on non-swell GFS wave fields with native 10-m U/V and appropriate shear/storm-motion fields, where the particles follow actual atmospheric 10-m wind rather than wave directions or shear vectors. Default particle density is higher than before, with performance caps on mobile. Right-click the tracer checkbox label (e.g. **10 m wind tracers** or **700 hPa wind tracers**) to adjust particle color, line thickness, and density (0.5–10.0×, default 5.0×) independently for each light/dark theme; Reset restores automatic speed-colored trails. The compact right-click editor displays the wind-speed color ramp in knots; disable **Color by wind speed (knots)** to choose a single fixed color instead. Switching colors immediately clears the previous fading trails; **Animation speed** stays adjustable in either color mode. These are **not** wave-direction unit vectors, storm-motion/shear barbs, or model-difference vectors. Missing components produce no tracers. Tracers are screen-space effects, with batched color/width strokes and a shared particle budget across panels. Animation and trail fading use elapsed time rather than monitor refresh rate; hidden tabs are paused. Tracers do not modify stored data or the existing PNG/GIF export layers.

Use the compact **Shortcuts** button next to the User guide for a quick keyboard, mouse, hover, and touch reference. The popup contains a printable **SHORTCUTS.pdf** link.

Right-click applicable labels in either menu to edit color, thickness, density and other available style settings. County boundaries are controlled solely by their checkbox, regardless of zoom. Cities display more places at closer zoom; for significantly more names, use the optional Python 3.6 GeoNames installer in `CITY_LABELS_INSTALL.md`. The separate Reset icon sits beside the Map overlays selector. ### Sounding profile completeness checks (v1.2.61)

When a native upper-air profile is missing for the selected model, initialization, or forecast hour, the chart continues to label its original six-level fallback as **sparse**. That fallback has no full dewpoint column and must not be interpreted as the model's native vertical resolution. When native surface pressure is available, below-ground isobaric levels are deliberately hidden; therefore the number rendered at high terrain may be less than the raw count stored for that cycle. The sounding status now explicitly reports removed temperature, dewpoint, and wind levels.

To diagnose a missing or incomplete sounding in the browser, open the sounding and run `EVUSoundingProfiles.getDiagnostics()` in Developer Tools. It shows the requested model/cycle/hour, matching Zarr path, raw and rendered level counts, missing native chunks, or why a profile hour was not advertised. GFS retains its native 37-level behavior when published; REFS Mean is natively much sparser and its unobserved levels must never be fabricated.

For a converter-side check of RRFS, HRRR, all five REFS members, REFS Mean, RAP and GFS, run `python3 audit_sounding_profiles.py --root zarr --models all` from the updated Converter v1.4.57 directory, using the true output root. Repair missing native chunks under the existing converter locks, and publish the profile catalog last. An updated viewer alone cannot reconstruct uncollected upper-air fields.

## Right-click: time series and sounding

**On a desktop, right-click a location on a model or analysis map; on a touch device, press and hold that location for about 0.6 seconds without moving your finger.** Then select one of these tools. Both open a floating chart window that you can move or resize. Use the **download icon** in the chart header to save a PNG. Open soundings and point time series change their chart colors immediately when the viewer switches between light and dark modes; saved PNGs reflect the active theme without reloading the chart data.

**Time series** plots the current field at the clicked location across the full forecast cycle. In the AI / GFS / GEFS model set, it samples **six-hourly** at selectable forecast hours (F000, F006, F012, etc.) through **F240**, and joins adjacent valid values. GFSv16/v17 uses **three-hourly** leads through **F240**; RRFS/REFS remains **hourly**. Genuine missing nominal forecast hours remain breaks in the line. Lines use a visible three-pixel stroke; no forecast values are interpolated or invented. It stays tied to the chosen model, field, location, and cycle, even if you subsequently change what is displayed in the viewer. Instantaneous quantities and run-to-date totals are shown as **lines**; time-period precipitation and snowfall accumulations (such as 3-, 6-, and 24-hour totals in GFS, with 1-hour totals also available in RRFS/REFS) are shown as **bars**. Rolling accumulation intervals can overlap, so adjacent bars should not be added together. Unavailable hours are not zero. The Y-axis uses rounded, whole-number ticks for most fields, retaining decimals for precipitation and other quantities where sub-unit differences matter. The data-availability note is separated from the forecast lead-time axis label, and the vertical/horizontal grid lines are visible in either display theme. It may take a moment to load the full series; progress is shown in the chart window. For Reflectivity with Ptype, the series plots reflectivity (dBZ), not the precipitation-type category.

**Sounding** displays a Skew-T / Log-P sounding at the clicked location, with **red temperature**, **green dewpoint**, correctly oriented **meteorological wind barbs**, a roomier **wind hodograph**, and a **model diagnostics** panel that includes both 0–1 km and 0–6 km bulk shear (when published). When available, it uses the newer pressure-level profiles collected by the converter, extending up to 100 hPa. The diagnostics box samples model-grid CAPE, helicity, and **0–1 km / 0–6 km bulk shear** values when published. These values are sampled independently from the published model grids, not calculated from the profile winds. Basic surface and level-summary information is also displayed. The Skew-T uses a practical −50°C to +50°C near-surface temperature axis and a header spaced above the plot. Hodograph wind-speed ring labels are kept clear of the cardinal directions. The dry-adiabat and mixing-ratio guides remain visible in both light and dark mode, while the valid date/time and latitude/longitude stay anchored within the chart during refreshes. The sounding **updates automatically when the forecast hour or cycle changes**, including during playback. Its chosen location and model remain the same.

An **approximate most-unstable (MU) parcel trace** appears as a dashed gold line when the native profile has enough temperature and dewpoint levels. It selects the highest equivalent potential temperature in the lowest 300 hPa of usable moisture data, lifting dry adiabatically to the LCL and approximately moist adiabatically above. Its starting pressure and LCL are noted below the diagnostics, and it is omitted for sparse or incomplete moisture profiles. It is a qualitative approximation: no parcel-derived CAPE/CIN or authoritative convective diagnostics are implied.

The sounding window reports how many temperature, dewpoint, and wind levels are available. **REFS Mean uses its own operational ensemble-mean profile**, including time-lagged members, but has relatively few available pressure levels. REFS member profiles may also have gaps. RAP analysis soundings are available at their corresponding hourly valid times when published. **GFSv16 and GFSv17** native profiles use published GFS pressure-level Zarr stores at the selected cycle and forecast hour; the GFS analysis panel instead samples the valid-time GFSv16 F000 profile. If the GFS store or selected hour is missing from `data/catalog_profiles.json`, the display explicitly reports a sparse fallback, not a complete sounding.

**Sounding limitations:** Full profiles may not yet be available for older cases or every forecast hour. In that situation the viewer uses the earlier, limited-level sounding when possible, and labels it as a sparse fallback. Profile grid resolution differs by model: RRFS/REFS profiles may be reduced-resolution, while the GFS profile store uses its native cropped 0.25° grid. None should be assumed to resolve local terrain or narrow weather boundaries. Some diagnostics may be unavailable, and any shown values are only as good as the published fields for that source and hour. With a compatible converter, native surface pressure allows below-ground isobaric levels to be excluded, and native 2-m temperature and dewpoint anchor the profile at the ground. Older profiles without surface pressure are clearly identified as not terrain-screened; missing levels are not invented. These soundings are best used for visual case-study exploration, **not operational parcel diagnostics or safety-critical decisions**. Desktop right-click and mobile touch-and-hold use the same data sources and open the same charts. On small mobile screens, the chart occupies most of the available screen width; the header has Close and Save controls.

## Fields, precipitation type, and snowfall

**Updraft helicity analysis comparison:** For the 1-hour model UH maximum, the Analysis panel displays the radar-derived **MRMS mid-level (3–6 km AGL) 60-minute rotation track**, ending at the selected valid time. For accumulated model UH, the Analysis panel displays the **gridpoint maximum** of successive 60-minute MRMS tracks from the beginning of the forecast through the selected valid time. The accumulated MRMS analysis is unavailable unless every required hourly track has been published. Model UH (m² s⁻²) and radar rotation track (×10⁻³ s⁻¹) are different physical quantities; the viewer provides **two independently labeled colorbars**, including on all-panel PNG/GIF exports. This is a qualitative comparison of rotating-storm signatures, **not observed UH**.

**Precipitation analysis selection:** All model sets use the same priority: **CCPA first, Stage IV second, MRMS third**, selecting the first complete analysis window. As additional verification is published, the viewer automatically upgrades to the highest-priority complete source. Stage IV for each 1-, 3-, 6-, 24-, or 48-hour precipitation interval, or cycle-to-date accumulated precipitation, **only if all required Stage IV hours are present**. Otherwise, it tries the next source in priority order. CCPA, Stage IV, and MRMS are never mixed within a total. If neither source has a complete interval, the panel reports unavailable data instead of filling gaps with zero. The active source is labeled on analysis images. The same source decision applies to point time series and difference maps; Stage IV comparisons require the published aligned-analysis files. MRMS remains the source for composite reflectivity and precipitation rate. Native multi-hour CCPA/Stage IV/MRMS products are used where appropriate; other periods are built from complete, non-overlapping intervals of one product, quality pass and grid. No missing hour is treated as zero.

**Precipitation-type labels are model-specific:** HRRR is **Derived precipitation type**, RRFS and REFS members are **Dominant precipitation type**, and REFS Mean is **Ensemble dominant precipitation type**. These exact terms appear in panel titles and per-panel PNG/GIF labels. The comparison-wide legend uses the neutral description **Precipitation type (model-specific)** because all models share one categorical color scale.

**REFS Mean PMM products:** composite reflectivity is displayed as **PMM composite reflectivity**. Accumulated precipitation is labeled **PMM accumulated precipitation** when the published field comes from the PMM precipitation source. The viewer does not infer PMM from a generic ensemble-mean field; older data stores may need the converter's targeted PMM precipitation repair before they contain the correct source data.

**Reflectivity with Ptype** appears below Precipitation type in the Radar menu. It colors model reflectivity by forecast precipitation type: green progressing into yellow/orange for stronger rain echoes, blue for snow, pink for freezing rain, purple for sleet, and amber for mixed precipitation. Both reflectivity and precipitation type must be available for the chosen hour.

**Precipitation rate** is displayed in inches per hour (in/hr), including map values, the colorbar, point time series, and PNG/GIF exports. Stored values are unchanged.

**Optional grid interpolation:** use the **Interpolate** checkbox beside Image opacity to apply GPU bilinear filtering to continuous scalar-field shading. It is **on by default for eligible GFS atmospheric fields**, but **off by default for GFS Marine weather and RRFS/REFS**. Marine, GFS atmospheric, and RRFS/REFS interpolation choices are saved independently in the current browser. This blends the four surrounding grid values for display only; it does not change stored Zarr values, point sampling, time series, sounding data, contours, or calculations. Categorical precipitation type and the hybrid **Reflectivity with Ptype** field retain nearest-neighbor rendering so category boundaries and codes are not blended. PNG/GIF exports use the same setting as the live panels.

Snowfall and precipitation fields include hourly, multi-hour, and accumulated options where available. Some totals need a complete sequence of earlier data, so a missing input can make a later accumulation unavailable. Missing data are **not** treated as zero.

For **NOHRSC snowfall** in the Analysis panel, 6-hour analyses are generally valid at 00/06/12/18 UTC and 24-hour analyses at 00/12 UTC. A 48-hour total uses two available 24-hour periods; the accumulated total adds consecutive 6-hour periods beginning after model initialization. There are no native 1-hour or 3-hour NOHRSC plots. Small, isolated snowfall artifacts are filtered from the map shading, without changing reported values.

**GFS marine weather:** The Marine weather menu in GFS comparison mode contains the six wave-height and wave-period scalar products available from both GFSv16 and GFSv17: significant combined wave height, wind-wave significant height, primary swell height, primary wave period, wind-wave period, and primary swell period. For **non-swell wave plots**, **10 m Wind** is enabled initially and draws actual GFS 10-m wind barbs from the same forecast store; **Wave direction** is off initially and draws independent wave-propagation arrows if enabled. **Swell-only plots** do not show unrelated 10-m wind barbs; their Swell direction arrows remain independently controlled and initially off. Both controls retain any later user choice. The field-specific Wave direction, Wind-wave direction, or Swell direction toggle controls native direction records; they are **not** meteorological wind barbs. **Right-click anywhere in the direction toggle box** to choose color, thickness, and density. These choices are saved separately from meteorological wind-barb appearance and apply to live arrows and captured arrows in exports. The v16 wave source is public AWS GRIB2 and the v17 wave source is local GRIB2. The global 0.25° wave grid reaches farther north than the former global 0.16° source, although ocean masking still differs from the atmospheric domain, so map regions outside wave coverage remain transparent/missing. Matching scalar wave products can be compared in the GFS difference panels. Circular wave directions themselves are not subtracted, and maximum individual wave height is not included because availability for both versions has not been established.

## Adjust the numerical colorbar

You can change the displayed range on most **numerical** colorbars without changing the underlying values.

1. **Click the numerical colorbar** to open the color-scale editor.
2. Move the **Minimum** and **Maximum** sliders, or type precise values into their adjacent entry boxes.
3. Select **Apply** to update the shading, **Cancel** (or Escape) to leave it unchanged, or **Reset default** to restore the original range. After applying a custom range, use the **circular-arrow reset icon** beside the colorbar title to restore defaults without opening the editor.

The full color palette stretches across the selected interval. For fields with a transparent near-zero interval, that single interval remains centered on physical zero as the minimum and maximum change; it does not drift with the original palette or create a second blank band. The new scale applies to panels showing that field and is included in exports. The **numeric minimum/maximum override** remains while stepping through forecast hours but resets on page reload. **CAPE colorbars use familiar round labels (such as 500, 1000, and 1500 J/kg)**. **Categorical precipitation-type legends cannot be adjusted** this way.

### Upload your own .pal color table

1. **Right-click a numerical colorbar** in an individual panel or the shared legend, or **click the main shared colorbar**. The color-scale dialog opens. On touch devices use an ordinary tap on the main colorbar.
2. Select **Upload .pal** and choose a plain-text GRLevelX/RadarScope-style table containing `Color:`, `SolidColor:`, `Color4:`, or `SolidColor4:` entries. Simple `value R G B [A]` or `value #RRGGBB[AA]` stops are also accepted. The file must have at least two distinct values; 64 KB is the maximum upload size.
3. Check the imported range and adjust **Minimum** or **Maximum** if desired. The table values are interpreted in the **currently displayed field units**, such as hPa, °F, inches, or dBZ. The viewer never silently converts units or changes forecast values.
4. The imported palette immediately updates other panels for that same field, its numerical colorbars, and PNG/GIF exports. It is saved per field in browser local storage, so it returns after page reload on that browser. It is **not uploaded to the server or synced between devices**. If browser storage is blocked or cleared, the import lasts only for the current session.
5. Choose **Restore built-in colors** to remove the imported table. The small reset icon beside the shared colorbar also restores its built-in colors and numerical range. Independent GFS precomputed difference and MRMS rotation palettes can be imported without replacing their corresponding model-field palettes. The separate floating RRFS/REFS difference window retains its fixed signed palette.

Imported table colors are not suitable for categorical precipitation-type legends, which remain fixed. Large files or malformed RGB/alpha values are rejected with an explanation.

## Overlays and transparency

**Default severe-weather colors:** CAPE uses additional gradients between 10 and 1000 J/kg, preserving distinct high-instability hues. Both SRH colorbars now render negative helicity in contrasting blue/cyan shades and positive helicity in yellow/orange/red-purple, with zero between them. GFS percent frozen precipitation is only plotted when its native value lies in the physically valid 0–100% range; invalid negatives are missing, not zero. Default State/Country outlines on light, low-speed 10-m wind, gust, and 300-hPa wind shading use a dark stroke with a narrow light keyline; individual saved colors can override the inner stroke.

The **Shear & helicity** field group displays 0–1/0–6 km bulk shear and
0–1/0–3 km storm-relative helicity (SRH). Default color scales are **0–60 kt for 0–1 km** and **0–90 kt for 0–6 km**; use the numerical colorbar to change them. On **bulk shear**, the **Shear barbs**
checkbox displays the upper-minus-lower wind vector difference as conventional
wind barbs, with 5/10/50-knot markings. Their shafts point opposite the
downshear direction, following wind-barb orientation conventions; the barbs
describe shear, **not** wind at a single level. On **SRH**, the **Storm motion barbs**
checkbox controls native Bunkers right-moving storm-motion barbs, also in knots.
All overlays follow synchronized panels, pan/zoom operations, and panel PNG
exports, and the severe-weather checkbox state is remembered independently
of other wind overlays.

The overlays require native U/V components in the published Zarr catalog for
that model and hour. If a source omits them, the corresponding overlay is
absent rather than simulated. The RAP analysis requires the latest converter
and missing-field recovery for older analysis hours to publish native 0–1 km and 0–6 km shear components. Native RAP 0–1 km components are available in Converter v1.4.1 when provided in the source GRIB inventory. A missing
barb overlay does not imply missing scalar shading.

Depending on the field, you can enable height or pressure contours, wind barbs, and contours of the shaded field. **Numerical CAPE, shear, storm-relative helicity, updraft helicity, also support shaded-field contours**, with conventional rounded value intervals that become finer at close zoom. Categorical precipitation-type fields do not support these numerical contours. **For 500-hPa absolute-vorticity shading, RRFS and individual REFS members use a modest display-only smooth, while HRRR and REFS Mean use an even lighter smooth** to make plots easier to compare. The stored fields and point-sampled values are unchanged. **Small, field-specific values near zero are transparent** where a clear background is meteorologically useful: below 10 J/kg for CAPE, below 2 kt for wind speed/components and bulk shear, within ±10 m² s⁻² for SRH, within ±5 m² s⁻² for updraft helicity, within ±5 × 10⁻⁵ s⁻¹ for 500-hPa absolute vorticity, and below 1% for snow cover/frozen-precipitation percentage. The exact neutral interval is tied to physical zero and remains a single interval when the numerical colorbar range changes. Relative humidity remains shaded down to 0% because very dry air is a meaningful meteorological signal. Difference maps also become transparent close to zero. This lets the underlying map background show through in dark and light modes, without changing the meteorological data. Temperature and MSLP shading remain continuous because zero is not a no-weather threshold for those fields. Live height and MSLP contours use slightly thicker, clean single strokes without a contrasting halo; the existing export contour appearance is unchanged. Dark-mode 500-hPa vorticity maps use brighter aqua geographic outlines by default to improve contrast with their vorticity colors, while custom outline styles remain in control. The available overlay checkboxes share compact rows when space permits and wrap when necessary. Report counts appear below the switches. The available options appear with the field controls. Use **Image opacity** to fade the weather shading without fading geographic outlines, contours, wind barbs, or observation markers. Applicable overlays are also included when exporting plots.

## Weather observations and storm reports

On all **Radar & precip. type** fields, enable **METAR** to display colored circles or **mPING** to display triangles. Their colors indicate reported rain, snow, freezing rain, sleet, or mixed precipitation. Reports are usually selected from within about 30 minutes of the forecast valid time.

On supported composite-reflectivity, shear/helicity, or severe-weather maps, enable **SPC reports** to display preliminary tornado (red), wind (blue), and hail (green) circles. The selected reports are shared by **all active panels**, including RRFS, HRRR, REFS members, REFS Mean, and Analysis, for the common forecast valid time. Select a report to see any available details. Reports are hidden by default.

On all **Radar & precip. type**, **Shear & helicity**, and **Instability & severe storms** fields, enable **GLM lightning** to display satellite-observed lightning flash centroids from the **preceding 60 minutes**, ending at the forecast valid hour. The age colors indicate **0–15 min (pink)**, **15–30 min (orange)**, **30–45 min (cyan)**, and **45–60 min (violet)** before that valid hour. The selected overlay remains enabled across fields and page reloads. The flash locations are satellite-detected optical lightning, **not individual cloud-to-ground strikes**; age is measured relative to the model valid hour, not the current wall-clock time. Choose an age-colored diamond marker to see its timestamp and originating satellite. Published GLM observation hours must be generated by the companion collector; if data are missing, the control reports **GLM data unavailable**, without implying there was no lightning. Converter 1.4.13 applies the configured retention period to unprotected local and published hours while preserving valid hours covered by protected historical cases. Age colors apply to live maps and panel PNG/GIF exports.

**Limitations:** Reports may be delayed, incomplete, revised, or absent. Future forecast hours cannot yet have verifying observations. A blank area does not mean no weather occurred. Some accumulated severe-weather fields display reports over a longer period than the most recent hour.

## Compare two panels

In **RRFS/REFS mode**, select the comparison checkboxes beside **two compatible panel titles** to open a **Difference** map. In **GFS mode**, the three difference panels are already included, and their datasets are precomputed by the converter. All difference panels show shaded signed differences and geography **without meteorological contours, wind barbs, height isolines, or temperature overlays**, irrespective of the normal panel toggles. For a floating RRFS/REFS comparison, the result is **first selected panel minus second selected panel**. On opening, that floating difference window adjusts its map area to match the selected panel's aspect ratio and geographic footprint, so both horizontal and vertical extents agree as closely as the screen permits. The floating window can be resized freely afterward. Counties are controlled by the independent Counties checkbox at every zoom level, including the floating difference window. Difference PNG exports preserve the checkbox state at capture time. Standard unrotated 2D difference-map PNGs also use a faster projection path without changing image resolution, field sampling, color scales or geographic accuracy; Globe and rotated views retain exact per-pixel projection. Saved difference maps use the same plot-area geometry as individual panel PNGs at the selected output size, reducing differences in geographic crop. Fit and Fill still determine whether edges are shown or cropped, and independently resized or zoomed maps retain their chosen view. Difference-map colorbars use whole-number labels for most fields; fractional labels are retained for small precipitation and other quantities requiring sub-unit precision. The same ticks appear in exported difference PNGs. The difference uses fixed field-specific near-zero transparent ranges and consistent signed colorbar limits through the forecast period. The difference follows changes to the forecast hour and synchronized panel view; you can zoom the floating map independently for close inspection. Use the window's download icon to save a PNG. Incompatible or missing fields cannot be compared.

## Save PNGs and animated GIFs

### Record an interactive expanded-panel video (desktop and Android; v1.2.57)

Expand a single model/analysis panel with the lower-right expand control. Use that panel's upper-right **Save** icon, choose **Record expanded panel (video)**, and click **Start recording**. When the browser asks what to share, select **this browser tab** rather than a window or screen. The recorder restricts capture to the expanded panel DOM, including its colorbar, map and panel overlays. The floating **Stop & save** recording control, external **Data overlays / Map overlays** menus, and large **numbered terrain panel-selection markers** remain visible while recording but are excluded from the saved video, even if they visually overlap the panel. The effects of those controls on the map (including enabling terrain, wind tracers and other overlays) **are recorded**. During recording, the numbered terrain marker is displayed outside the captured element and returns to its normal place when the session ends. The recorder will never silently capture the entire tab if panel isolation fails. A large **3, 2, 1** countdown appears before recording begins.

Perform normal interactions while recording: zoom/pan, change the forecast hour or field, rotate/tilt, enable terrain, wind tracers, geographical layers, reports and similar overlays. The floating red **REC** timer and **Stop & save** button remain accessible outside the recorded panel region. Clicking **Stop & save** ends the video and triggers a local `.webm` download (or `.mp4` if the browser's recorder supports it); no audio is captured. The panel-save/expand icons are hidden during recording, and collapsing the panel automatically ends the recording. Click **Cancel** during the countdown to discard the pending capture without creating an empty file.

**Desktop Chrome/Edge:** Requires HTTPS, Element Capture (`RestrictionTarget` / `restrictTo`), MediaRecorder, and explicit browser-tab sharing. Select the current tab. The recorder never falls back to unisolated full-tab or desktop recording.

**Android Chrome (new in v1.2.57):** Mobile Chrome does not provide desktop tab sharing/Element Capture. When the expanded panel is active, Video export instead uses locally generated **canvas recording** if `canvas.captureStream()` and `MediaRecorder` are supported. It composes the live MapLibre map with the visible terrain-weather layer, contours, wind barbs, wind tracers, geographic and city overlay canvases, a readable panel label, and the shared colorbar. MapLibre-rendered observation symbols appear through the map canvas; HTML-only report popups are represented as simplified text rather than an exact reproduction. Menus, Stop & save, terrain-selection numbers, and device UI are excluded. There is no browser sharing prompt, microphone capture, or server upload. Recording is bounded to approximately 960,000 pixels and up to 15 frames/s to protect mobile memory and battery. Keep the viewer in the foreground; switching to another app automatically ends and saves an active recording. Continue to use the existing 3–2–1 countdown and Stop & save workflow. Mobile browser support and encoding performance vary; use short clips if the device becomes sluggish.

**Android pan/zoom recording stability (v1.2.58):** The mobile video recorder now stages and checks each candidate frame. While terrain rendering pauses for a gesture or redraw, it retains the last complete video frame rather than inserting a blank/black one. Once a new map and terrain/field image is available, recording continues with the updated view. Because Android's canvas recorder cannot capture the browser's retained/composited frames directly, a gesture may appear as a brief still frame followed by the updated location; desktop video continues to capture fluid map movement through Element Capture. For diagnostics during a recording, open the browser console and run `EVUPanelRecorder.getStatus()` to inspect accepted and held frame counts. No new options, permissions, exports, or converter processing are required.

Both recording methods save locally as WebM or MP4, depending on available codec support. They record the live display, not the separate presentation-size PNG export. The mobile method is a first implementation requiring verification on actual Android devices.



Use a panel's **download icon** to export one plot, or **Export all panels** for a comparison image or animation. All exported PNGs and GIFs receive a unique UTC timestamp and short suffix in their filenames, so repeated downloads keep distinct names instead of browser-added `(1)` and `(2)` suffixes. Choose the image proportions (**16:9** or **4:3**) and whether to **Fit** the entire map area or **Fill** the frame (which can crop edges). Titles, times, legends, and applicable overlays are included. Exported METAR, mPING, and SPC Local Storm Report markers are slightly enlarged for presentation readability; on-screen marker sizes do not change. Geographic outlines in PNG and GIF exports retain their separate two-tone outline treatment: a contrasting casing with a themed inner stroke. **Live maps use one clean stroke without the export halo.** This keeps coastlines and political boundaries visible over **both dark and bright weather shading** in either viewer theme while preserving a cleaner appearance than the older export-specific treatment. This applies to individual, all-panel, and difference-map exports. County visibility in PNG and GIF captures follows the Counties toggle, not the zoom level; appearance and visibility are preserved during high-resolution exports.

Select **Large labels** when preparing images for presentations. It enlarges the model or analysis title, time information, and exported colorbar title and tick numbers in exported PNGs and GIFs, without changing the live viewer. The difference-map title and time line use the same normal and large font sizes as individual-panel exports.

For animated GIFs, the defaults are:

| Resolution | Default range | Maximum frames |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Standard (1200 px) | F000–F024 | **25** |
| High (1600 px) | F000–F024 | **25** |

Both resolutions use a shared **25-frame maximum** for single-panel and all-panel GIFs. The default delay is **700 milliseconds per frame**. You can change the forecast-hour range, step, and delay. The final frame stays visible slightly longer. Larger or longer GIFs may take more time to export.

Analysis panels use titles such as **Analysis · NOHRSC · 24-hour snowfall**, with the **valid time** alone on the subtitle.

## Data availability and troubleshooting

- **A panel says missing:** The selected model, variable, hour, or verifying analysis may not be available. Try another hour or source. Some accumulated totals require every contributing period.
- **An analysis is missing:** Its valid time may not be supported, the analysis may not be published yet, or an earlier required period may be missing. RAP is an hourly model analysis and is only shown for supported fields at valid hours that have been collected and published. The site administrator can check a specific case with the RAP diagnostic page.
- **Changes do not appear after deployment:** Refresh the page to load the latest viewer.
- **A plot or map fails to load:** The viewer includes a **Diagnostics** page for technical troubleshooting; contact the site maintainer if the issue persists.

The viewer is intended for forecast evaluation and comparison, not for issuing official warnings. **All displayed times are UTC.**



### Sounding surface wind vectors

Native soundings use **2-m temperature and dewpoint** and **10-m U/V wind** at the model's native surface-pressure anchor, in addition to the available native isobaric levels. The levels readout counts a surface wind level only when **both** 10-m vector components are present. This is a display anchor at surface pressure, not a claim that 10-m wind was measured at 2-m height. Older profile stores need the converter's surface-only upgrade before the new level is available. Below-ground pressure levels remain excluded.


Sounding-chart footnotes explain that the MU parcel uses an **approximate pseudo-adiabatic path** and does **not** compute CAPE/CIN; the terrain-screening note reports whether native surface pressure was available. These footnotes appear on separate fitted lines beneath the right-hand diagnostics card. The Skew-T axis tilt has also been moderated slightly.

Wave-height default colorbar maxima are 40 ft for combined significant waves and 30 ft each for wind-wave and primary-swell height. These are adjustable display defaults; they do not cap stored values or change the signed difference colorbars.


### Snow-to-liquid ratio control (v1.1.71)

For snowfall accumulation products derived from WEASD or TSNOWP, use the **Snow-to-liquid ratio** slider beneath the snowfall-source radio buttons. It defaults to 10:1 and supports 5:1 through 40:1 in 0.5 steps. The selected ratio is saved for subsequent sessions and applies to both GFS and RRFS/REFS derived snowfall products. Snow-depth change does not use an SLR.

### Sounding export provenance

Sounding PNGs include the actual model source, model initialization in UTC, forecast lead (F###), valid time in UTC, and selected location directly within the saved image. The exported filename also records initialization, lead, and valid time. Analysis panels use the actual analysis source and cycle rather than borrowing the forecast-panel run.


### Detailed self-hosted roads

The preferred Roads overlay uses optional self-hosted U.S. Census TIGER/Line primary and secondary highways (Interstates, U.S. routes and state routes), with a simplified Natural Earth fallback. Build the tiles using `install_tiger_highways.py` and `HIGHWAYS_INSTALL.md`. Legacy OSM guidance is retained only for installations that already use it. Preserve the road-source attribution for whichever tiles are deployed.


## AI / GFS / GEFS comparison (v1.2.16)

Six panels: AIGFS, AIGEFS Mean, HGEFS Mean, GFSv16, GEFS Mean and valid-time GFSv16 F000 Analysis. Native AI means are plotted at 0.25° where available; GEFS Mean uses its native 0.25° surface and 0.5° upper-air streams, without artificial upscaling. The shared timeline uses 6-hour steps through F240. A field is shown only if that forecast's native GRIB inventory and published Zarr catalog provide it.

Upper-air selections include **250/300 hPa Height/Wind**, **500 hPa Height/Vorticity**, and **700/850/925/1000 hPa Height/Temperature**. These use the same shading, contours, barbs, map outlines and export styles as equivalent GFS or RRFS/REFS fields. The AI model-set menu intentionally omits 2-m dewpoint and specific humidity. Native 700/850 hPa isobaric dewpoint/specific-humidity *contour overlays* may appear when their source provides same-level moisture.

For upper-air plots, choose the **Shaded image / difference** radio option within Data overlays: **250/300 Wind speed or Height; 500 Vorticity or Height; 700/925 Temperature, Height, Dewpoint, or Specific humidity; 850 Temperature, Height, Wind speed, Dewpoint, or Specific humidity; 1000 Temperature or Height**. The selected shaded image determines the signed difference in the GFS precomputed panels and in RRFS/REFS or AI dynamically created comparisons. Dewpoint and specific-humidity contour overlays are independent of the image selection. Differences require both native components, valid time matching and consistent grids (AI regular-lat/lon grids are geographically aligned before subtraction).

Wind tracers use true matching U/V wind components from the current forecast valid time. The viewer can locate a complete pair in a same-cycle companion catalog dataset; it does not mix grid resolutions, forecast cycles, or unrelated fields. On bright or dark temperature plots, the speed-colored tracer stroke uses a subtle contrasting edge. If 850 or 1000 hPa native vectors are absent from a model's catalog, no tracers can be drawn until those records are ingested and published.
